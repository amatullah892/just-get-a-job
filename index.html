BEHIND THE STORY

The facts.
The credits.

A fictional character illustrates a documented reality. No scene is presented as a real person’s testimony. All numerical claims below are sourced; the film’s words and interactions are creative interpretation.

Research

[1] Employment and homelessness

Australian Institute of Health and Welfare (2025), Specialist homelessness services annual report 2023–24: Clients who are working and receiving specialist homelessness services. Around 10,000 working clients aged 18+ were homeless at the start of support in 2023–24. This does not count every employed person experiencing homelessness, and does not imply all clients sleep rough.

Read the AIHW research

[2] Hours and wages

Fair Work Ombudsman, Minimum wages and Hours of work. From 1 July 2026 the published National Minimum Wage is $1,004.90 a week before tax. A full-time ordinary week is 38 hours. The rate generally applies to adult employees not covered by an award or registered agreement; awards and other entitlements may differ.

The scrubber distributes the published weekly amount across 38 hours. It is an explanatory calculation, not a payroll calculator or an assertion about the character’s wage.

Minimum wage source · Hours source

[3] Housing affordability

Anglicare Australia (2026), Rental Affordability Snapshot: National Report, seventeenth edition, pp. 7–10. Surveyed 48,776 advertised listings on 14–15 March 2026. For a single minimum-wage earner, 255 were affordable and suitable: 0.5%, rounded.

The study uses a 30% income affordability benchmark, appropriate household size and eligible benefits. Our 200-mark visual shows the rounded percentage, not an application success rate. This March study predates the July 2026 wage benchmark above; we do not recalculate the study using the later wage.
